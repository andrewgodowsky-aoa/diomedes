# Diomedes: marketability, income scenarios and commercial execution

Version: 2026-09-10.1
Date: September 10, 2026
Status: Founder planning assessment, not a guaranteed earnings forecast or public customer claim.
Related pricing: docs/business/PRICING_STRATEGY_2026-09-10.md, version 2026-09-10.3.

## Bottom line

Diomedes has a credible path as configurable software introduced through practical setup and implementation. It is neither automatically doomed by large AI vendors nor already validated because small businesses buy other software. Services-assisted software adoption is a third route between selling consulting hours forever and waiting for anonymous self-service subscriptions.

My planning judgment is to target approximately $10,000-$40,000 of first-12-month revenue while maintaining a deliberate part-time sales/delivery schedule, not to budget personal spending against that money. Zero or low revenue remains possible. A stronger roughly $90,000 revenue scenario can be constructed, but requires much more selling, delivery capacity, a working paid product and repeatable support. No evidence supports assigning reliable probabilities yet.

The product's long-term upside is not bounded by a solo consultant's hours if workflows become reusable, the software earns renewals and other people can deliver/support it. Those are conditions to prove, not reasons to declare future income certain.

## Which parts of the outside critique survive scrutiny?

Useful: acquisition matters; founder time/support is a real cost; an unsigned experimental application adds buyer friction; recurring revenue requires retention; returned salaried time is not automatically payroll savings; unknown custom integrations need scope control; production customers need credible continuity and data controls.

Not established: zero software revenue for 18 months, single-digit self-service sales, universal pilot overruns of 2-3 times, guaranteed losses on $299 work, universal $175+ consulting acceptance, quoted warm/cold conversion rates, an order-of-magnitude vertical-sales advantage, and a fixed solo revenue ceiling. These are unsupported judgments in the supplied text, not measured Diomedes data.

Incorrect technical claim: EV signing does not confer instant SmartScreen reputation. Microsoft explicitly states that EV no longer bypasses SmartScreen. Valid signing establishes publisher identity and contributes to reputation; a new signed binary may still warn. Enterprise policies can block unsigned files, but not every small business universally refuses every unsigned prototype. [1]

Overcorrection: a locally sold product is not immune to competition. Jobber already advertises quote drafting, follow-ups, automations and AI reception inside an operational system. A buyer need not know the phrase AI agent to choose an overlapping feature in software they already use. [3]

Relationship correction: the founder's Triangle hospitality connections and a remodeling prospect are potential introductions and validation settings, not evidence of signed customers, approved data access, no sales cycle or an endorsement. Do not publish their names/logos as clients without permission. Geography is North Carolina for the current opportunity, not assumed Chicago operations.

## One configurable product, focused first purchases

Andrew's thesis is cross-industry: generic work primitives plus configuration and bounded industry-specific connectors, mappings, skills and rules. Examples include intake, classification, retrieving records, comparing data, preparing a quote/draft, assigning work, following up, producing a brief and escalating an exception.

A restaurant operations brief and a remodeler's open-items brief can share much of that machinery. Differences in data meaning, permissions, units, APIs, workflow rules and acceptance tests are still real. Do not promise that every business is supportable through configuration alone before adapter behavior is proven.

The commercial discipline is to focus each offer on one recurring job, not to constrain the entire company to one industry. It is reasonable to test two different business settings early to discover which primitives genuinely transfer. It is not reasonable to promise ten bespoke production stacks simultaneously.

Locally delivered SaaS describes an acquisition and support approach. It does not necessarily mean local inference, an offline license or cloud-hosted execution. The customer buys configured software that remains useful after the consultant leaves; setup services help adoption and expose repeatable product needs.

## Marketability: plausible, not validated

The strongest early prospect has repeated coordination across two or more data sources, a responsible buyer, accessible lawful data, costly delays or omissions, and a willingness to change a small process. The weakest prospect has a rare task, no clear owner, locked-down data, no feasible budget, or an existing system that already solves the problem when configured properly.

A May 2026 Census analysis of December 2025-May 2026 data found reported AI use around 17-20% overall and no significant increase over that period among firms with fewer than 20 staff. That supports neither the claim that every small business already has sophisticated AI nor the claim that the rest are ready buyers. Adoption friction and uncertainty are part of the market. [2]

The initial differentiator is installation, fit, cross-system workflow execution, understandable controls, results that survive normal failures, and someone accountable for the agreed setup. The durable differentiator would be reusable connectors/workflows, verified policy enforcement, measured reliability, low servicing cost and evidence that others can implement the same product. Merely having many selectable models or copying a competitor's feature list is not enough.

Self-service distribution should remain a long-term route. Services-assisted subscriptions can start earlier, once the paid workflow and access controls are real; there is no justified rule that software must earn zero for a fixed 18 months. Conversely, listing $99 on a website does not create a customer acquisition engine.

## Pricing conclusion

Retain the approved entry ladder: free first hour; $100/h agreed continuation; $299 Quick Start; $350 audit; $249 local plan; from $749 local deployment; from $1,250 bounded pilot; from $2,500 broader implementation; planned $99/month Business; optional Managed from $249/month.

The outside critique is right about scope risk, not about replacing every entry price. A 12-hour $1,250 reusable pilot has about $104 of revenue per delivery hour before sales, overhead and follow-up. The same price for 35 delivery hours yields about $36/hour. The scope and delivery method determine the economics. A $299 setup taking three hours is not automatically a loss; one taking two days is not sustainable as the default.

Use larger custom quotes when new integrations, data cleanup, site work, multiple stakeholders or production reliability engineering make them necessary. A $2,500-$7,500 custom implementation range is a proposal consistent with the existing from-$2,500 line, not an across-the-board public price increase. Do not force regulated industries into a cheap local-install offer just because they care about privacy.

A possible $39-$49 starter is an experiment, not approved pricing. It needs bounded features and very little human support. It should not undercut $99 Business while providing the same workload and expectations. Basic safety remains foundational at every price.

These are arithmetic scales, not acquisition forecasts. They require continuing renewals and capacity to serve the accounts. Recurring managed revenue has human service obligations and should not be valued or described as pure software ARR. One-time implementation revenue belongs outside these recurring totals.

At 100 Business accounts and 30 Managed, the modeled basic support is 25 hours/month and extra managed work 45 hours/month, before onboarding, incidents, sales or software engineering. Higher support usage or incident clusters can dominate margin. Hiring changes the capacity ceiling, but wages, coordination, training and quality checks must enter the cost model.

## Unit economics: support can matter more than tokens

At $99/month, an illustrative $15 API bill, $5 hosting, $2.97 collection cost and 15 minutes of $50/hour support leave $63.53 before fixed product costs, sales and profit. With 1.5 hours of that support instead, only $1.03 remains. These are assumed costs, not actual measured gross margins.

At $49 with the first set of assumptions, only $15.03 remains. This is why a low-cost software entry can work while an inexpensive, high-touch managed service cannot. A reusable onboarding path and dependable execution are financial features.

One uninterrupted job still can need support if authentication expires, a vendor changes a schema, a device sleeps, context loses a rule, an external result is uncertain, or an update regresses behavior. Rule injection, enforced scopes, drift checks, bounded corrections, verification and recovery are near-term prerequisites to the low-babysitting promise. Model confidence or a self-review alone is not evidence of success.

Before hiring, measure collected contribution and a backlog that can be delegated. An implementation/support contractor is a plausible first addition if delivery is the bottleneck, not a universal hiring rule. A 20-hour/week contractor at an assumed $40/hour costs $41,600/year before any other costs. Do not hire against projected gross revenue alone or assume contractors remove the founder's review responsibility.

## Readiness work that improves the commercial case

1. Distribution: a consistent verified publisher, protected signing keys, timestamped releases, trusted update metadata, installer/upgrade/uninstall/recovery evidence, and a clean-machine Windows acceptance pass. EV is not a reputation shortcut. Store distribution is a possibility only after eligibility review. [1]
2. Paid access: a real company-controlled API gateway, server-verified account/organization entitlements, per-customer quotas/spend caps and revocation. A public client paid flag cannot guard the company key. Billing webhooks require validation and duplicate/out-of-order handling; payment, identity and capability authorization remain distinct. [4][5]
3. Ownership continuity: individual identities, a business owner/admin, transferable administration, revoke a departing manager, export configurations/history, and tested offboarding. Do not anchor business access to the founder's or one employee's personal credentials.
4. Deliverable workflow: one useful operation with typed inputs/outputs, enforceable scope, drift and postcondition checks, budgeted correction, graceful stop, recorded uncertain effects and restart behavior. Then repeat a comparable operation in another business context without copying the core into a separate product.
5. Operating agreement: accepted scope, lawful data access, roles, retention/deletion, backups/restore expectations, incident contacts, support limits, applicable vendor terms, and appropriate professional legal/insurance review before production-risk commitments. A local model is not a compliance certification.
6. Measurement: paid conversion, active use, 30/60/90-day renewals, net client time returned, avoided expense or collected contribution separately, implementation hours, support per account, measured inference cost, and failure/escalation rates.

This is an execution checklist, not a claim that these items are built by this documentation pass. Do not create a second Trust system simply to attach billing. Manual service invoicing can start before self-serve subscription checkout; it cannot substitute for secure control of a shared paid inference route.

## Practical next validation

Use the free fit hour to find a repeated operational burden and a person authorized to sponsor it. A conversation is a lead, not consent to access business data. Seek initial examples in two settings, such as hospitality and remodeling/construction administration, while keeping the shared workflow family narrow.

After a few fit calls, sell or explicitly waive a small scope in writing; record the real fee collected and reason for any waiver. Measure the current manual process first. Run synthetic/export-based tests and a shadow comparison before trusted production use. Track new review/rework as part of the new process. Charge only for an agreed deliverable; do not turn a free call into an unannounced invoice.

A useful internal 90-day test is a handful of paying, repeat-using accounts, at least two comparable workflow deployments and documented renewal/referral feedback. Exact targets are management decisions, not industry benchmarks. If most implementations require unique code or ongoing founder rescue, narrow the supported workflow or change the quote before recruiting many more customers.

## Website consequences

Explain the recognizable job and the result before the term harness. Show services-assisted setup and the software as two related offers, not one confusing all-inclusive fee. Make the actual first hour free, retain the approved price anchors, disclose what is excluded, and mark future software/hosted features planned.

Use three business examples, not a restaurant-only website and not an unsupported claim to integrate everything. Do not use prospective clients' names/logos or imply design-partner status. Keep personal/power-user product access visible so the business page does not redefine the entire product.

An illustrative capacity example using $40/hour is easier to maintain than stale occupational statistics: 5 net hours/week x 52 x $40 = $10,400/year. Business plus Managed is $4,176/year before setup and excluded costs. That is a capacity comparison, not a guarantee that wages fall or a quoted ROI. Show the formula, not an invented "average time saved."

A public trust explanation can say, "You choose the boundaries. Diomedes works inside them," with the current release's limitations made clear. Retain the end-state of no routine babysitting while distinguishing actual supported automatic actions from prototypes, planned controls and manual service work.
