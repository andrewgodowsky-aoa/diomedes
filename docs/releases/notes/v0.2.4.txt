WHAT IS NEW
  A Home built around your jobs, a Work view with the board beside the thread,
  credits charged by tier, jobs that check in before they spend more, and the
  Nectovia Agent on your own Personal work with credits you buy.

  Home is built around your jobs. Its rail lists Needs your input, Working and
  Finished, each with its count, and a row opens that job's thread, decision
  or card. When the business has more than one project, a line at the top of
  the rail switches between All projects and each project. The ask box sits
  under the greeting, over the newest exchange, and Earlier opens the rest of
  the conversation.

  Home shows up to two tasks a Nectovia run proposed and left in the Inbox,
  newest first. Accept moves a task to the Board, where it starts only when
  you start it, and Not now hides it on this computer.

  Home and the Nectovia thread pin the newest bar, line or area chart a reply
  carried, with a caption saying when it was written. While a step runs, a
  line sweeps along the chart's baseline, and it stays still when reduced
  motion is on.

  Once the Nectovia conversation has started, Home's ask box has a thread's
  row in place of the Style box: Engine, Model or tier, Effort and Agent. Its
  Engine menu lists the AI tools a conversation can run on.

  The Agent menu no longer grays out every worker when the conversation is on
  the Nectovia Agent. Only workers that lead with Build or Fix are grayed, and
  they say why: the Nectovia Agent doesn't take those modes yet.

  A project's top bar has a switch, Nectovia | Work. Work replaces Architect,
  and a project you kept in Architect opens in Work. The view choice left the
  ··· menu, and Settings, Appearance and Ctrl K name the two views the same
  way.

  In Work, the board sits beside the thread: Needs your input, Working, Up
  next and Finished today, under the open count and Open full board. A card
  opens its thread, and a decision opens where it waits. The thread's own Team
  and Loop run stay at the top of that column, and Activity and Recent are no
  longer in it.

  On the free version, projects open in Work and the Nectovia tab shows a
  lock. It opens a short notice with Sign up for a plan, Remind me later and
  Don't remind me again. The view you chose before is kept, and it comes back
  with a plan.

  Automations are called Routines everywhere you read them, the rail,
  Everything, Ctrl K and Settings included. Routines is one of the rail's
  default pins, and on the free version its row says Paid plans.

  The team view stays on every plan. On the free version you lead the team,
  and Nectovia's seat shows that it can lead it on a plan.

  Without a plan, you can buy credits and use the Nectovia Agent on your own
  Personal work while they last. In Personal, Settings, Usage shows the
  credits you bought and a buy box that buys for you alone. The free version's
  notice says you can buy credits. Leading a team, Routines and a Nectovia
  worker or advisor in a loop still need a plan. When the credits run out,
  work stops at the end of the step in progress and keeps what it did.

  In a business with no plan, the Nectovia Agent still needs a plan, and
  owners and admins are told so wherever they start it. The buy box there says
  the credits wait for a plan.

  Nectovia Agent work is charged in credits at the rate for its tier:
  Efficient, Focused or Thorough. The rates come from a credit price table
  Nectovia publishes, and they're the same whichever model does the work. A
  job's estimate before it starts uses them too.

  Jobs check in instead of stopping at a cap. When a job reaches its check-in
  amount, it finishes the step in progress, saves, and asks whether to keep
  going. Keep going lets that one job use the same amount again, and Stop here
  keeps what it did. A loop that reaches its amount waits in Needs you, where
  Keep going starts it again.

  Nectovia's check-in amounts are 100 credits for Efficient, 250 for Focused
  and 500 for Thorough. Owners and admins can set their business's own amounts
  in Settings, Usage, under Job check-ins, and a change applies to jobs that
  start after it.

FIXED
  A loop run waiting to pick up where it left off no longer starts working
  while Nectovia closes. It picks up the next time Nectovia opens.

UPDATES
  Settings > App updates offers stable releases only. Nothing is downloaded,
  verified or installed until you confirm it, and a download is accepted only
  when its SHA-256 matches the digest the release publishes. An installed
  0.1.7, 0.1.8, 0.1.9, 0.1.10, 0.1.11, 0.2.0, 0.2.1 or 0.2.3 on Windows can
  find this release when you press Check for updates.

  An update from 0.1.9, 0.1.10, 0.1.11, 0.2.0, 0.2.1 or 0.2.3 keeps your
  settings, routes, saved keys, projects and conversations. An update from
  0.1.7 or 0.1.8 that skips 0.1.9 still gets the one-time fresh start 0.1.9
  introduced: the earlier version's data and the Documents\Diomedes folder are
  set aside, renamed with "before 0.1.9" and the date, and nothing is deleted.

  Your install is refreshed the first time this version opens, and again after
  each later update. Settings you never changed move to the new defaults,
  anything you chose is kept, cached screens are cleared, and a byte-for-byte
  backup and a record of what moved are kept. A custom look keeps its colours,
  fonts and artwork, while text sizes, line spacing and density follow the new
  version. You see it once: Updated to 0.2.4, with how many settings moved.

  Saving AI routing and privacy settings needs 0.2.3 or later, because the
  account service no longer accepts them from 0.2.1. A choice saved in 0.2.1
  keeps working under its earlier rules until you save it again.

  This release has no Mac build. A Mac keeps the version it has, and Check for
  updates there doesn't offer this Windows release.

LIMITS
  This build is unsigned and experimental. On Windows, SmartScreen may warn
  before the installer runs: choose More info, then Run anyway, after checking
  the file's SHA-256.

  Settings > Account and the Nectovia Agent's AI service were tested only
  against stand-ins for the Nectovia account service. A real sign-in has run
  on one installed Windows copy of a test build made before 0.2.3. Two sign-in
  fixes came after it: finishing without a second press, and the page the
  browser tab ends on. Both were tested only against stand-ins. The account
  service this version uses is still a staging service with a fixed testing
  budget: while it is, the Nectovia Agent's answers are kept short, and the
  Agent stops answering once that budget is spent.

  The account service hasn't published models or credit prices for any tier
  yet. Until it does, work on a Nectovia tier is refused with a note that the
  tier has no Nectovia model right now, and nothing is charged. Work on your
  own AI tools isn't affected.

  The page in the browser tab needs port 47319 on your computer. If another
  program is using that port, the tab stays on a loading bar as before, and
  sign-in still finishes in the app. If you quit Nectovia before you finish in
  the browser, the browser says it can't connect. Open Nectovia and sign in
  again.

  The Norton change was checked with Norton on one Windows computer. Other
  antivirus programs weren't tried.

  Signing in needs the Nectovia account service, so if it can't be reached
  when Nectovia starts, you can't open anything, your own projects included;
  the sign-in screen says why. A connection lost after you sign in doesn't
  sign you out.

  Buying credits isn't available yet. Until payments are set up on the account
  service, Settings, Usage answers that buying isn't available right now, and
  buying has run only against a stand-in payment service. Pay as you go can't
  be used until then.

  Once buying opens, credits you buy without a plan run the Nectovia Agent in
  Home's conversation. This version still shows you Nectovia locked in the
  Nectovia | Work switch and grayed in the Engine menu.

  Pay as you go, credit prices, check-ins, credit limits, bought credits,
  Individual credits and the paid-access changes were tested against stand-ins
  and test databases, not against the live account service.

  This version has no screen for setting a member's monthly limit. Unless one
  is set, a member's limit is the plan's whole allowance.

  When an Individual plan ends, Personal Agent work is refused, but the
  default conversation doesn't move back to your own AI tool the way a
  business's does.

  A routine that reaches its check-in amount waits in Needs you without Keep
  going. Start it again to go on.

  Routines has a New routine button that can't be used yet.

  Under All projects, when the business has more than one project, Home shows
  no suggestions and no Finished group.

  The new account routing has not yet completed a real signed-in,
  paid-provider acceptance run. Automated routing tests use synthetic model
  responses. A route without current qualification or required privacy consent
  stays unavailable.

  Apart from a few conversation turns on one outside engine and a connection
  check on the company's AI account, nothing in this version was tried against
  a real provider.

  Board work on the Nectovia Agent, including work a request starts in
  Automatic mode, was tested with stand-in model responses only. Helpers and
  teams don't run on it.

  Team cards and hand-offs were tested with stand-in tools only.

  The phone apps can't reach this computer through the relay yet; this version
  only connects the computer and lists it as online. The relay was tested
  against a stand-in and hasn't yet run on the live account service.

  An update that installs this version over an earlier one, including the
  change from Diomedes.exe to nectovia.exe, hasn't been run on an installed
  copy.

  Routines run the weekly brief alone, and only while this computer is on.
  They tell you inside the app and send nothing by push, email or phone.

  Resume, Retry, Fork and Steer work on Codex runs only.

  Run checks run only the checks a task declares, and never run your project's
  own code. Nothing checks a run automatically when it finishes.

  Files has no PDF viewer. Pictures and PDFs cannot be sent to an AI, and a
  binary file cannot be put back from an earlier version.

  Trigger rules don't watch conversations, Ask or Plan. Tool rules don't reach
  Codex, Claude Code, OpenCode or other outside tools, which run their own
  tools, and on every outside tool except Codex a text rule reads the text
  after secrets are removed, so a rule looking for a secret may not fire
  there. A correction asked of a tool other than Codex was not tested, and a
  rule that asks a work loop run to correct still can't reach it: the
  correction is recorded and shown as not sent.

  Delegates that change files in their own copy, and a lead that runs a team
  of them on your own connected services, are built but can't be started from
  the app yet, though the Loop run dialog can now add a Nectovia worker and
  advisor. The Console still has no History screen and no Make tasks from this
  plan.

  Every route except the Nectovia Agent still runs on, and is billed to, your
  own account. A provider connection you saved yourself works only for a
  business whose plan includes the Nectovia Agent, or for Personal work on an
  Individual plan. You can still choose such a connection for a thread, and
  Settings has no place to change or remove it.

  The local model needs a server that answers llama.cpp's props request, so
  Nectovia can tell which profile is running. Without it the model shows as
  running, and work that names a profile is refused.

  Prompt caching and the route checks were built and tested against stand-ins.
  No call reached a provider while building them, so whether a provider honors
  Off shows only in a route check on your own connection.

  The local model can't be chosen for a Team member or a loop run from the app
  yet.

  The Nectovia worker and advisor in the Loop run dialog were tested only
  against stand-ins, a scripted local model server and the offline scripted
  account service, never a live Nectovia tier or the live account service.
